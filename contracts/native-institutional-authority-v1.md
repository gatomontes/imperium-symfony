# Native institutional authority protocol v1 — P0–P3

This is a proposed deployment policy with a positive offline implementation. It grants no real institutional authority by being committed or installed. Real enrollment, evidence attestations and each institutional act require separate owner authorization. The prior A0–A3 implementation remains accepted within preparation/refusal scope, including its admission-test qualification. Its old verifier remains permanently refusing; this is a separate versioned protocol.

## Trust and effects

Deployment-owned Ed25519 public trust belongs only to `IMPERIUM_NATIVE_INSTITUTIONAL_V1`, role `NATIVE_INSTITUTIONAL_CUSTODIAN`, one exact instance and the six enumerated effects: `ADOPT_ROSTER`, `SUPERSEDE_RECRUITER`, `RETIRE_ROSTER`, `REVISE_GARRISON`, `REVOKE_DECISION`, `REVOKE_ISSUER`. This custodian is an explicitly proposed policy issuer, not a new Office or a root with general operational powers. Enrollment separately confirms SHA-256 of the 32 public-key bytes and exact domain/effect/validity/writer-boundary policy. Even a key already enrolled for Formation or ProtectedMission needs this independent enrollment. There is one enrollment per store; key rotation/re-enrollment is unsupported and requires a separately reviewed migration.

Ordinary incoming commands cannot enroll trust. A deployment custodian with filesystem/CLI access is the enrollment authority; possession of a fingerprint string does not prove human authority. Restrict this administrative access in a future deployment. The unsigned owner policy template retains unknown identity, public key and times as null. No default selects a genuine owner or installation.

Signed payload keys are exactly `schema`, `domain`, `instance_id`, `trust_fingerprint`, `issuer_role`, `effect`, `object_digest`, `issued_at`, `expires_at`, `nonce`. Schema is `imperium.native-institutional-decision/v1`; digest is SHA-256 of existing `CanonicalJson::encode(object)`; signature is base64 Ed25519 over canonical payload bytes; nonce is 48 lowercase hex. The verifier uses only enrolled public bytes, checks all scope and time fields, and consults issuer/decision revocation inside the same transaction as effect publication. Incoming public keys and extra envelope fields refuse.

## Roster and provenance

Each object binds `expected_head` to the exact current registry head. Null means the verified enrolled empty registry, not a caller's general claim of absence. Act, registry, roster and authority-revision identities are distinct from occupancy generation.

`ADOPT_ROSTER` binds `seat`, `actor`, `occupancy_generation`, null `prior_roster`, `evidence`, `effective_at`, `expires_at`, and `expected_head`. Supported Seats are ordinary Recruiter and Garrison Constable. Recruiter evidence must be the exact structurally valid A0 projection; Garrison evidence must equal the sole retained native occupancy in the fixed local source root. Initial adoption is an explicit custodian attestation plus a prospective registry decision. It does not authenticate historical MasterMason execution or enlarge an old qualification. The original evidence and its limitations are retained with the signed act; public roster output contains its digest and `historical_producer_authenticated: false`.

`SUPERSEDE_RECRUITER` requires a current Recruiter, exact previous roster digest, a different actor, occupancy generation plus one, and `evidence: {source_id, source_digest}` for the owner's exact prospective succession case. This establishes only the roster act under the proposed custodian policy; it does not claim an authenticated historical qualification, formation delegation or general recruiting powers. Garrison succession is unsupported; this campaign extends one existing occupant, never fabricates a second ACTIVE binding. `RETIRE_ROSTER` binds the exact active roster/actor/generation and null evidence. Retirement remains recorded; re-adoption cannot erase it. Roster expiry and revocation invalidate current resolution without deleting history.

`resolve` rechecks enrolled trust, active roster, current time, revocation and (for Garrison) exact retained original occupancy under the native lock. Its result is an observation at the stated registry/frame/time boundary, not portable proof of indefinite currentness. `snapshot` reports history even when expired or revoked; it is not a currentness certificate. Historical private projections stay structural after enrollment.

## Garrison authority and admission

`REVISE_GARRISON` binds `expected_head`, `roster_digest`, `prior_revision`, exact A0 `request`, and original `occupancy`. The signed request extends only the two existing narrow `GarrisonAuthorityRequest::EXTENSION` scopes. The actual registry resolves null as verified absence only at the matching head; later requests must bind the exact prior revision ID/digest. Original occupancy bytes, actor and generation remain unchanged. Other observed powers retain their original values; no reservation, Profile handoff, selection or execution power is imported from newer defaults.

Decision nonce consumption, request replay fencing and the distinct immutable authority revision are co-published in one journal frame. Exact signed-input replay returns the original completed act after later expiry/revocation; different input under that nonce refuses. Reusing a request nonce in another prospective revision refuses. Historical replay rechecks the original signature and validity at its retained accepted time; current authorization is never renewed by replay.

For a fresh `apply` or `admit`, the acceptance instant is one clock sample inside the shared lock, after loading retained state and excluding exact completed replay, immediately before fresh authority checks. All decision/trust, roster/currentness and effect-interval checks for that operation use this instant. Every retained `accepted_at` uses the same value; an act's returned timestamp equals its enclosing retained act timestamp. Admission retains its instant alongside the exact returned disposition and authority witnesses in the same frame. It does not revalidate using a different timestamp while constructing or publishing that frame. Acceptance is not the later filesystem rename time, an external trusted timestamp or a promise that physical publication precedes signed expiry. The lock keeps cooperating authority writers excluded until publication. An interval already expired at acceptance refuses before an effect. A failed pre-publication attempt retains no acceptance and a retry must validate afresh; a completed effect recovers against its original authenticated acceptance evidence, without extending any interval. `resolve` and `inventory` likewise use one locked observation instant for currentness and returned observation time.

The actual `SubordinatePersonaCanonicalAdmissionService::admit` routes to the native protocol once the deployment is enrolled. It validates a complete canonical Guildhall delivery, current roster/original/revision and exact two powers, then co-publishes custody and disposition in the native journal. Revision alone does not admit a candidate. A separately invoked admission is an institutional act; this campaign invokes it only on synthetic deliveries. The retained native disposition identifies original binding, roster and authority-revision digests. It is not formation eligibility or an appointment. Exact completed admission recovery checks retained signatures, witness validity and revocation ordering and returns the same evidence without another effect. Changed delivery/binding or duplicate candidate refuses.

Native custody is held in the aggregate journal, not materialized into partially written legacy files. `GarrisonInventoryResponseService::respond` reads it under the same lock together with intact existing custody records, preserving original independent inventory power and refusing duplicate Persona identities. It emits `imperium.native-garrison-inventory-response/v1` on stdout/return. Legacy Guildhall delivery of this new response schema is not implemented. Other consumers have an explicit completion boundary: they cannot consume the new aggregate as legacy raw files.

Fresh admission and inventory use one combined custody identity policy: every native and retained legacy record must be intact, have the supported custody schema, `ADMITTED_HELD` state, the enrolled instance and a nonempty string `persona_id`; Persona identities must be unique across both stores. Invalid or already-conflicting custody refuses with `NAT040_CUSTODY_INVALID`. A fresh admission for an already held Persona refuses with `NAT037_CANDIDATE_ALREADY_ADMITTED` before any frame or consumption/effect change, even for the original legacy delivery or another delivery identity. A different Persona may be admitted and inventoried alongside the unchanged legacy record. Legacy completion is not imported as native completion: there is no automatic migration, replacement disposition or new legacy recovery protocol. Exact native replay still returns only its retained completed effect.

## Exclusion, fencing and limitations

`NativeBoundary` uses the existing `AtomicTransition` lock with one new scope, `native-institutional-authority-v1`. Enrollment, roster/currentness, revocation, revision, admission and inventory share it. The 38 mapped legacy services use it too. StateStore lock order is native boundary then bootstrap lock; read/write stop once the native store exists, while the separately authorized structural exporter can still take the bootstrap observation lock. All mapped T04-dependent Conscription entry points and raw Garrison occupancy consumers stop with `NAT002_LEGACY_WRITER_OR_READER_FENCED` after enrollment, except the two supported native consumer routes. Before enrollment they retain legacy behavior under the common lock. A directory left by uncertain enrollment also fences legacy work.

This explicitly disables unsupported old workflows under an enrolled deployment; it is not a transparent migration of every legacy lifecycle. The owner must approve that fence, quiesce external/administrative writers and use this exact code on all cooperating processes. Old binaries, direct file edits, reparse aliases, unrelated stores/writers and administrator rollback are outside the guarantee. Do not claim authoritative control over them. The retired smoke entry remains unavailable. Formation's operator-root witness adapters and retained-publication verifier are unchanged and do not accept native protocol records.

Frames have consecutive generation filenames, exact predecessor digests, bounded JSON, exclusive temporary creation, full writes, `fflush`/`fsync` and one rename publication point. State/effect/consumption share that point. Missing generations, competing names and corrupt links refuse. A durable `.pending` artifact is an unknown outcome fence, not an instruction to commit, delete or refund; no automatic pending-file repair is provided. Before-write interruption permits the still-valid exact operation to retry; after-rename interruption permits historical recognition. These are process/filesystem semantics, not an OS sandbox, administrator-proof ledger, trusted timestamp or power-loss/directory-fsync guarantee. Capacity is 1 MiB per frame and 1,024 frames; exceeding it refuses before another effect and requires a reviewed migration.

No provider, real keys, private installed state, deployment or mission execution belongs to P0–P3. Default formation transport, CF01 terminal refusal, CF02 authenticated completed-publication recovery, IR01 closure and settled interview/drafting/mission boundaries remain intact. Readiness, activation and execution remain false.
