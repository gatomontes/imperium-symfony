# Opportunity: Governed Internal Software Factory

## Status

Idea for commercial validation. This document does not amend Imperium doctrine, authorize implementation, or place product concerns inside the constitutional runtime.

## Signal

CIO Dive reports that enterprises are scaling agentic AI for internal software development while attempting to control technology spending. Nearly one-third of respondents in the cited McKinsey research said their organization declined to purchase at least one software feature because it could now be built internally.

The same signal suggests a change in the developer's role: less exclusive emphasis on writing code and more responsibility for directing, examining, and managing AI-produced software.

Source:

- Paige Gross, “Enterprises bet on agents to build in-house software, boost productivity,” CIO Dive, August 27, 2026: https://www.ciodive.com/news/enterprises-bet-agents-software-productivity/829016/

## Opportunity

Agentic coding makes internal software easier to produce. It does not make the resulting software institutionally safe to request, authorize, adopt, operate, maintain, or retire.

If enterprises replace purchased software with rapidly generated internal applications, they risk recreating shadow IT at machine speed:

- undocumented ownership;
- duplicated internal systems;
- unbounded repository or infrastructure access;
- ambiguous requirements and acceptance standards;
- uncontrolled dependencies and licensing exposure;
- credentials assembled beyond the immediate task;
- code deployed without an accountable adoption decision;
- unclear maintenance and incident responsibility;
- forgotten applications that continue to hold data or authority; and
- no defensible explanation of why the software was built or allowed into operation.

The commercial opening is therefore not another coding agent.

> Imperium can provide the governed institutional machinery through which internally generated software is commissioned, built, examined, authorized, adopted, operated, and retired.

## Commercial thesis

Do not sell “AI that writes software.”

Sell:

> An internal software factory that allows enterprises to build more of what they need without surrendering institutional control over what gets built, who may touch which systems, what evidence is required, and who may place the result into operation.

Code generation is becoming a commodity. The defensible value lies in governing the complete institutional act surrounding it.

Imperium already contains relevant experimental machinery:

- mission definition and planning;
- separately opened and claim-bound authority;
- temporary Delegates commissioned for bounded work;
- separately governed tools and credentials;
- least-assembled-power constraints;
- institutional decision records;
- independent examination;
- explicit human authorization;
- transactional adoption;
- interruption and recovery evidence;
- custody and provenance; and
- separation of cognition from external consequence.

## Product concept 1: Imperium Software Foundry

The Software Foundry would govern the lifecycle of internally commissioned software, including:

1. receive an operational need from a department;
2. establish the problem, intended users, constraints, and success criteria;
3. determine whether software is actually required;
4. authorize a bounded build commission;
5. commission only the specialists needed for the work;
6. grant repositories, tools, credentials, and environments separately and temporarily;
7. generate architecture, code, tests, documentation, and deployment materials;
8. subject the result to independent functional, security, maintainability, and business-fit examination;
9. return defects for revision without silently expanding scope;
10. obtain the required human decisions for consequential transitions;
11. adopt the artifact into operations through a separate lifecycle;
12. preserve ownership, maintenance, evidence, and retirement obligations; and
13. measure realized cost and value after adoption.

### Explicit boundaries

A successful build must not imply:

- approval of the original business case;
- authority to alter production;
- authority to deploy;
- authority to migrate authoritative data;
- authority to procure services;
- authority to communicate externally;
- operational adoption;
- authorization for follow-up development; or
- indefinite access to repositories, credentials, or infrastructure.

“The tests pass” is evidence about the artifact. It is not institutional permission to use it.

## Product concept 2: Build-or-Buy Tribunal

A narrower product may be easier to commercialize first.

The Build-or-Buy Tribunal would examine a proposed software purchase or internal request and produce a defensible recommendation:

- buy an existing product;
- build internally;
- extend a system already owned;
- integrate existing capabilities;
- automate only part of the workflow;
- postpone the decision pending evidence; or
- do nothing.

### Examination surface

The recommendation should consider:

- actual business requirement;
- available internal and commercial alternatives;
- purchase and implementation cost;
- expected internal build cost;
- model and compute cost;
- integration complexity;
- security and privacy exposure;
- data migration and custody;
- licensing and dependency risk;
- maintenance burden;
- required expertise;
- vendor lock-in;
- operational criticality;
- recovery and continuity;
- expected useful life;
- measurable savings or revenue; and
- uncertainty in every material assumption.

The resulting decision record should show who supplied the evidence, which standards were applied, what was rejected, who held decision authority, and what new authority—if any—was created.

### Relationship between the products

The Tribunal can become the intake gate for the Software Foundry.

- If the disposition is **buy**, the evidence supports procurement without granting purchasing authority.
- If the disposition is **build**, the approved case can become the basis of a separately opened development mission.
- If the disposition is **extend** or **integrate**, access remains limited to the exact affected systems.
- If the disposition is **do nothing**, the avoided project becomes measurable value.

This prevents the Software Foundry from becoming a machine that assumes every request deserves software.

## Candidate customers and work

The strongest buyers are organizations with meaningful software spending, recurring departmental requests, capable internal IT personnel, and growing use of coding agents.

Candidate work includes:

- internal dashboards;
- reporting and reconciliation tools;
- workflow applications;
- departmental portals;
- legacy-system adapters;
- data-entry utilities;
- approval and intake systems;
- narrowly scoped SaaS-feature replacements;
- maintenance of internal applications; and
- controlled modernization of repetitive manual processes.

The first pilots should avoid safety-critical systems, autonomous financial execution, regulated decisions, and destructive production migrations.

## Recommended first pilot

Use Imperium internally to commission one small but real business application or SaaS feature replacement.

A suitable pilot should have:

- a known current cost;
- one identifiable business owner;
- bounded data;
- no irreversible automated consequence;
- measurable handling time;
- a small integration surface;
- deterministic acceptance tests;
- a clear maintenance owner; and
- an existing commercial alternative for cost comparison.

The pilot should begin with the Build-or-Buy examination. The internal build should proceed only if the decision survives that examination.

## Evidence package

Each completed software commission should preserve at least:

- originating need;
- approved scope and exclusions;
- build-or-buy analysis;
- requirements and acceptance criteria;
- authority grants and consumption;
- repository, tool, credential, and environment custody;
- architecture and dependency decisions;
- generated and human-authored changes;
- examination findings;
- defect and revision history;
- security and licensing review;
- deployment recommendation;
- human authorization;
- operational adoption disposition;
- ownership and maintenance assignment;
- interruption and recovery evidence;
- retirement conditions; and
- realized-versus-expected value.

## ROI surface

This application gives Imperium a more defensible value surface than generic claims of productivity.

Measure:

- avoided software licensing expenditure;
- avoided implementation and consulting expenditure;
- cost of internal development;
- time from request to disposition;
- time from authorized commission to viable artifact;
- time from viable artifact to operational adoption;
- developer and reviewer hours;
- model and infrastructure cost;
- defects caught before adoption;
- unauthorized actions refused;
- duplicate or unnecessary projects rejected;
- maintenance cost after adoption;
- incidents and recovery time;
- expected versus realized savings; and
- useful life of the internal application.

Productivity is an intermediate signal. Financial and operational consequences are the result.

## Business model

Potential revenue layers:

1. **Build-or-Buy assessment**
   - fixed-price examination of one requested purchase or internal build;
   - defensible decision record;
   - no implied procurement or implementation commitment.

2. **Governed development commission**
   - priced by bounded mission, milestone, or delivered capability;
   - includes evidence, examination, and adoption preparation.

3. **Software Factory subscription**
   - recurring platform and operational fee;
   - governed intake, execution, evidence retention, reporting, and integrations.

4. **Internal software portfolio governance**
   - continuing ownership, maintenance, cost, access, dependency, and retirement oversight.

The customer should pay for governed business capability and defensible decisions, not tokens or lines of generated code.

## Differentiation

Avoid:

> We use multiple AI agents to develop software faster.

Prefer:

> We govern the complete institutional lifecycle of internally generated software—from the original need and build-or-buy decision through bounded construction, independent examination, operational adoption, and accountable retirement.

The central distinction is simple:

> A coding agent can produce an application. It cannot, by producing it, decide that the enterprise should own, trust, deploy, operate, or continue paying for it.

## Relationship to other ideas

This opportunity complements the existing enterprise Office and blast-radius concepts.

- The enterprise Office thesis packages governed execution as recognizable business functions.
- The blast-radius thesis constrains the consequence available to each participating agent.
- The Software Foundry applies both to the growing enterprise practice of replacing purchased features with internally generated software.
- The Build-or-Buy Tribunal supplies the institutional decision boundary before development begins.
- Transactional adoption prevents artifact completion from silently becoming operational use.
