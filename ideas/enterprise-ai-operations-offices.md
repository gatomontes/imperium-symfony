# Opportunity: Imperium-Powered Offices for Enterprise Work

## Status

Idea for commercial validation. This document does not amend Imperium doctrine, authorize implementation, or place product concerns inside the constitutional runtime.

## Signal

A recurring enterprise-AI argument is that most business workloads do not require the largest or most expensive model. Common workloads include:

- document summarization;
- structured information extraction;
- record classification;
- content generation;
- internal knowledge retrieval;
- customer-service assistance;
- code generation and review; and
- bounded automation of well-defined business processes.

At enterprise scale, the economic problem is not merely whether a model can perform a task. It is whether the additional capability of a more expensive model produces enough additional business value to justify its cost.

## Commercial thesis

Do not sell generic AI features. Sell governed execution of specific enterprise work.

> A governed workforce that selects the least expensive adequate model, performs sensitive enterprise work under bounded authority, escalates only when necessary, and preserves evidence of what it did.

The defensible advantage is not model routing alone. Routing will become a commodity. The advantage is model selection inside an executable, governed lifecycle in which eligibility, custody, validation, escalation, authority, consequence, recovery, and evidence remain distinct.

The commercial surface should consist of recognizable business functions. Imperium remains the underlying operating system.

## Product concept

Package domain-specific **Imperium-powered Offices**. Each Office is a deployable vertical work package containing:

- defined tasks and acceptance criteria;
- workflow and checkpoint contracts;
- model-eligibility and escalation criteria;
- authority and custody boundaries;
- deterministic validation rules;
- human-review thresholds;
- evidence requirements;
- integration adapters;
- cost, quality, refusal, and escalation metrics; and
- recovery behavior for interrupted work.

A typical job would:

1. accept a bounded business task;
2. classify its complexity, sensitivity, and consequence;
3. select the least expensive eligible model;
4. perform the work within a bounded commission;
5. validate the output mechanically and, where required, cognitively;
6. escalate uncertain or high-risk cases to a stronger model;
7. request human authority only where consequence requires it;
8. preserve an audit-ready evidence package; and
9. report cost, quality, refusals, escalations, and business value.

## Candidate Offices

| Office | Enterprise work | Likely buyer | Imperium advantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Document Intake Office | Extract, classify, summarize, validate, and route documents | Accounting, legal, insurance, healthcare | Provenance, custody, validation, exception handling |
| Knowledge Desk | Search approved policies, manuals, contracts, and prior cases | Operations, HR, support | Source-bound answers, citations, abstention, access boundaries |
| Service Copilot | Assist employees handling customers | Retail, veterinary supply, SaaS, hospitality | Recommendation without silent acquisition of decision or transaction authority |
| Content Operations Office | Produce drafts, variants, descriptions, and campaigns | Marketing teams and agencies | Approval lifecycle, brand constraints, source tracking, economical model routing |

## Recommended first commercial pilot

Begin with an **Accounting Document Intake Office**.

Reasons:

- the work is repetitive and expensive;
- business value is easy to measure;
- many outputs can be checked deterministically;
- low-risk and high-risk stages can be separated cleanly;
- ambiguous cases provide a natural escalation path;
- the workflow fits existing relationships with accounting firms; and
- the Office can prepare work without silently filing, sending, approving, paying, or modifying authoritative records.

### Candidate pilot workflow

1. Receive a client document into bounded custody.
2. Identify the document type.
3. Extract required fields.
4. validate formats, identifiers, totals, and completeness mechanically.
5. identify inconsistencies or missing evidence.
6. summarize the document or case.
7. route routine work forward.
8. escalate ambiguity or material defects.
9. prepare a human-review packet.
10. preserve the decision and execution evidence.

### Explicit boundary

The pilot must not imply that document understanding grants filing, payment, approval, communication, ledger mutation, or other consequential authority.

## Secondary pilot

The existing APM concept can become a **Product Research and Recommendation Office** embedded in the POS.

A cashier describes a customer situation. The Office researches plausible product categories, checks available inventory, explains its reasoning, recommends options, and identifies cases requiring expert or veterinary attention. It assists the cashier without presenting a diagnosis or acquiring sales, medical, pricing, inventory-mutation, or transaction authority merely through recommendation.

## Business model

Monetize three layers:

1. **Workflow implementation**
   - map one expensive, repetitive process;
   - establish task, evidence, authority, and integration boundaries;
   - configure the first Office.

2. **Recurring governed-execution subscription**
   - platform fee;
   - usage or completed-work pricing;
   - evidence retention;
   - integrations;
   - reporting and operational support.

3. **Reusable vertical Office packages**
   - deploy the same proven Office across customers in one industry;
   - configure customer-specific policies and integrations without rebuilding the constitutional machinery.

Prefer pricing by document, completed case, governed transaction, or monthly operational capacity. Avoid exposing token consumption as the primary customer value unit.

## Validation metrics

The pilot should prove:

- cost per completed case;
- percentage completed by the lowest-cost eligible model;
- escalation rate by reason;
- deterministic validation pass and failure rates;
- human-review minutes saved;
- correction and rework rates;
- unsupported-answer or abstention rate;
- interrupted-work recovery success;
- duplicate-consequence prevention;
- evidence completeness; and
- business-cycle time before and after adoption.

Model savings alone are insufficient. The Office must demonstrate that lower inference cost did not merely move expense into review, correction, incident response, or operational uncertainty.

## Positioning

Avoid:

> Imperium can do enterprise AI.

Prefer:

> Give us one expensive, repetitive business process. We will determine which parts can be performed by cheaper intelligence, which require escalation, which require human authority, and what evidence must survive every execution.

## Strategic sequence

1. Accounting Document Intake Office.
2. APM Product Research and Recommendation Office.
3. Internal Knowledge Desk for small and midsize businesses.
4. Software Maintenance Office for internal use and eventual commercialization.
5. Model Economics Console after multiple Offices generate real routing and outcome evidence.

## Next validation step

Select one real accounting workflow and capture:

- current inputs and outputs;
- authoritative systems involved;
- human roles and approvals;
- average monthly volume;
- current handling time and cost;
- common exceptions;
- irreversible or externally consequential actions;
- acceptable error and abstention thresholds; and
- evidence required to defend the result.

Only after that discovery should an MVP boundary and commercial price be proposed.
